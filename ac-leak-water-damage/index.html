AC Leak Water Damage in Salt Lake City: What to Know

All day long an AC system wrings humidity from indoor air, and the resulting water needs a way out. A pan under the coil catches the drips, and from there a thin pipe empties the pan. When that line clogs with algae and dust, the pan fills and spills over. With an air handler in the attic or a hallway closet, the overflow goes straight into the ceiling, wall or floor around it.

People tend to underestimate what a small, steady leak can do. The water wicks into drywall, soaks the platform under the unit and creeps under flooring, often with mold already started by the time a stain shows. Tracking the full extent of the moisture, drying it and making repairs is our part. You will still need an HVAC technician to clear the drain or fix the unit, and we can work around their visit.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut the System DownNo more condensate forms once the AC is turned off at the thermostat. Where the wiring on the air handler is getting wet, the breaker should also go off.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsCeiling lights, outlets and the unit itself are off limits while wet, and stay that way until their circuit has been turned off.
  3. Clear Water From the PanUse a wet-vac or rags to take the standing water out of the pan and stop the spill, provided the pan is safe to reach. Do not crawl across a wet attic to do it.
  4. Soak Up What You CanPress towels into damp carpet, mop hard flooring, put a bucket below any drip from the ceiling, and clear furniture, clothes and boxes out of the closet or away from the stain.
  5. Bring In Two ProsCall us to check how far the water spread, and call an HVAC technician to clear the drain line. Keep the AC off until the repair is made.
What We Do

Our AC Leak Water Damage Process

1

Find Every Wet Area

With thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team checks the unit, the adjacent walls or ceiling and the floor beyond the closet, so wet materials are found even where no stain shows.

2

Remove Water and Wet Drywall

Standing water in the pan, the platform and the carpet is extracted. Saturated or moldy insulation and drywall are removed, which opens the cavity to dry air.

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on for a few days, usually 3 to 5, and we log the moisture readings. Access to the air handler is kept open for your HVAC technician.

Days-old condensate is not clean water, so everything it reached is treated with an antimicrobial. After that, our crew puts back the drywall, insulation, baseboard and floor and paints the repair to blend in.

The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • A missing or failed float switch that should have shut the system off.
  • Low refrigerant or a clogged filter, either of which leads to a frozen coil.
  • A condensate pump that burned out or lost power and stopped moving water.
  • Algae, slime and dust from the coil plugging the condensate drain.
  • Bare refrigerant lines or ducts that sweat in humid weather and drip on the ceiling.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Drywall Below the Air Handler

An attic air handler leaks onto the drywall beneath it. First comes a stain, next a sag, and after days of soaking a piece may drop into the bedroom or hall underneath.

Insulation Around the Unit

Insulation around an attic unit soaks up overflow and holds it against the ceiling. Wet insulation loses its value and often has to be replaced.

Nearby Flooring

From the closet, water slips under the door and works below hardwood, laminate or carpet pad. Hardwood cups when it is not dried, while laminate swells for good.

The Platform and Closet Walls

Closet units often sit on a wood platform over the return air box. Water rots that platform and wicks up the surrounding drywall, where it stays hidden behind the unit.

Insurance

What About Insurance?

Coverage for an AC leak usually comes down to timing. If the pan overflowed suddenly and you acted when you found it, many homeowners policies pay for the resulting water damage. Denial is possible when the adjuster concludes the leak ran for weeks or months, since that reads as a gradual or maintenance problem. The air conditioner repair is typically excluded in both cases. Read your policy, and ask us for photos and moisture readings to support the claim.
