Burst Pipe Emergency Guide in Salt Lake City, UT

Home / Blog / Steps to Take When a Pipe Bursts in Salt Lake City, UT

Few plumbing problems rattle a homeowner like a pipe that suddenly bursts. At 4 to 8 gallons every minute, one broken pipe can soak whole rooms, damage what you own, and compromise the structure in a matter of hours. What you do in the first few minutes after finding the break often decides whether you face a simple repair or a full restoration, and it can keep thousands of dollars of damage off the bill. Salt Lake City Restoration put together these steps so Salt Lake City, UT residents know exactly how to respond to this common problem.

Step 1: Stop the Water at the Main Valve

Before anything else, cut off the water, because nothing you do afterward matters as much as stopping the flow quickly. Every minute the water keeps flowing adds to the damage and the cost. Start by finding the main shutoff valve for the house. Look by the water meter first, since that is where Salt Lake City houses tend to have it, often in a utility room, garage, or basement, or on a street-side exterior wall. Turn the valve clockwise (righty-tighty) to shut off water flow to the entire house. Some lines have their own local shutoff, so if you can tell which pipe failed, turn off that valve also.

Can't find the valve, or can't get it to turn? Phone the water utility right away and ask them to stop service at the meter. Knowing where that valve lives ahead of time, not during a crisis, matters for exactly this reason. Go look for it today, then check that the handle turns without a fight. Should the handle be seized, get a plumber out to fix it well ahead of any emergency.

Step 3: Protect Your Property and Belongings

Before the crew arrives, you can cut your losses, starting with getting documents, electronics, and valuables out of the wet zone. Lift furniture off wet carpet by placing aluminum foil or plastic blocks under legs. Pick up any area rugs lying on wet flooring. Use towels to soak up whatever standing water you can reach. Prop cabinet and closet doors open so air can move through. Turn off electricity to affected areas at the breaker panel if you can do so safely without standing in water.

Do not step into pooled water anywhere close to outlets, electrical panels, or appliances. Can't get to the breaker panel safely because of serious flooding? Leave the power as it is until trained help arrives and assesses things. No belonging is worth risking an injury.

Step 4: Document Everything for Insurance

Your insurance claim will need evidence, so record everything carefully before any cleaning starts. Shoot video and photos showing the standing water, every damaged material, and any belongings that were hit. Note the date and approximate time you discovered the burst pipe, and document the source of the leak if visible. This documentation, combined with the detailed reports Salt Lake City Restoration provides, will support your insurance claim and help you seek fair compensation for the damage.

How Salt Lake City Homeowners Can Avoid Burst Pipes

Insulating bare pipes in crawl spaces, garages, attics, and other unheated areas is a big step Salt Lake City homeowners can take, even though no measure prevents every burst. A slight drip from your faucets in a cold snap keeps the water in motion. Warm room air should be able to reach pipes on exterior walls, so open the cabinets under those sinks. Pressure over 80 PSI calls for a pressure reducing valve, so check your reading from time to time. Swap old supply lines for braided stainless steel, starting with any rubber hoses on the washing machine. And know your shutoff valve location before you need it.
