Kitchen Water Damage in Salt Lake City

No room has as many water connections as the kitchen, with its faucet, drain, disposal, dishwasher and refrigerator line. Nearly all of them are hidden inside a cabinet or behind an appliance. A drip from any of them travels to the rear of the cabinet base, then spreads beneath the flooring. Our Salt Lake City crew locates the leak, dries what it wet, and keeps as much of your kitchen intact as we can.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Plywood and solid wood will often dry successfully, whereas a particleboard box puffs up quickly and never returns to size. Toe kicks come off and a few small, concealed holes are drilled so dry air can travel behind and below the cabinets, which lets cabinets and countertops stay put in many kitchens during drying.

Signs to Look For

  • Cupped or raised floorboards, wood or laminate, near the dishwasher door
  • Sounds of dripping or hissing with all taps closed, or an unexplained rise in the water bill
  • Side panels on the base cabinets that are flaking or swollen at the bottom
  • Soft or discolored vinyl flooring near the refrigerator or along the cabinet line
  • Stale, damp air that hits you when the doors under the sink are opened
  • Water stains or bubbled paint on the wall behind the sink or dishwasher
Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

First hour

Water Runs Under

Water from a failed hose or fitting flows to the back of the cabinets and under the appliances. Seams and edges let it get below the flooring.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Begin to Swell

Particleboard cabinet bases and toe kicks soak up water and swell. In the dark, enclosed gap below the cabinets, and at the back of the dishwasher, mold can start.

Day 3 to 7

Flooring Starts to Move

As adhesive releases, vinyl begins lifting, while hardwood boards cup and laminate edges swell. Drawers and cabinet doors stop lining up.

Past one week

Removal Becomes Likely

Swollen cabinet boxes cannot be brought back. The subfloor may be coming apart in layers, and mold behind cabinets often means they come out together with the countertop.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Stop the Source

First we close the line to the leaking appliance or fixture, slide out the dishwasher or fridge when needed, and identify the exact failed connection so the repair can happen ahead of drying.

2

Measure the Wet Area

We check the cabinet bases, the wall at their back and the floor throughout the room with thermal imaging and moisture meters. Under kitchen flooring, water often goes farther than anyone expects.

3

Dry Without Demolition

With the toe kicks off, air gets pushed below the cabinets and behind them. Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, and we treat damp cavities with an antimicrobial.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

Cabinet sections, flooring or subfloor that cannot be saved are taken out carefully. After dry readings are confirmed, the appliances go back in and we help you arrange the remaining repairs.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Under the Base Cabinets

Every side of the space beneath base cabinets is closed off. Water collects there, stays for weeks, and grows mold that nobody sees until the cabinets are pulled.

Wet Wall Behind Cabinets

The wall at the back of the sink and dishwasher holds drain and supply pipes. Cabinets fully cover that drywall and framing, so a leak can soak them and mold can spread out of sight.

Flooring Over Flooring

Kitchens often have new flooring laid over old. Water gets trapped between the layers, where it cannot evaporate and will not show up on the surface.

Behind the Dishwasher

A slow drip at the supply or drain connection wets the subfloor and the cabinet sides next to it. Until the floor changes, the dishwasher covers everything.
